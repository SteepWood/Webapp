Joinery timber species in Australia range from a dressed Tasmanian Oak board at [about $30.25 per lineal metre ex-GST](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) to NSW Spotted Gum at [about $44.00 per lineal metre ex-GST](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) for the listed reference sizes in August 2026. Those numbers are material inputs, not installed cabinet prices. The practical decision is whether the timber should be solid, veneer or an engineered timber-look finish, then which species suits the colour, movement, wear and budget of the room.

## The short answer

Choose Spotted Gum for a strong Australian hardwood presence, Blackbutt for warm pale-to-mid tones, Tasmanian Oak for a lighter adaptable interior timber, and American White Oak for the imported-oak grain many contemporary projects seek. None is automatically the “best” for every kitchen or staircase. The board size, grade, finish, construction method and local humidity matter as much as the species name.

| --- | ---: | --- |

| American White Oak, 19 × 140 mm | [$30.55](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) | Imported-oak appearance and contemporary furniture |

| Tasmanian Blackwood, 19 × 165 mm | [$59.40](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) | Richer furniture and feature-panelling work |

The figures are dated and dimension-specific. A 32-millimetre board, a clear grade, a wider board or a longer length can change the input price materially. Always read the timber schedule, not only the species heading.

## How timber is priced, and why ex-GST per metre confuses people

Timber suppliers commonly price boards per lineal metre for a nominated dressed size, often excluding GST. A cabinetmaker does not buy a finished kitchen by simply multiplying that number by the room length. The board needs to be selected, transported, allowed for waste, cut, machined, joined, sanded, finished and installed. Wide solid panels also need a construction detail that allows natural movement.

Consumer-facing prices should be shown as a total including GST under [Australian display-price guidance](https://business.gov.au/legal/fair-trading/display-prices). That is why an installed joinery quote and an ex-GST timber list should never be compared line for line. The timber list tells you relative material inputs. The quote tells you the finished, measured scope.

Timber cabinetry sits at the top end of published joinery finish bands: [about $2,200–$4,000 or more per linear metre installed](https://www.leadkit.com.au/blog/kitchen-renovation-cost-sydney-2026). That includes far more than boards. It also explains why a timber veneer door can be the smart choice where the visual effect is wanted over a wide run without paying for solid timber through every component.

## Australian hardwoods: Spotted Gum, Blackbutt, Tasmanian Oak, Jarrah, Victorian Ash

Spotted Gum has a recognisable figure and strong Australian identity. The listed 19 × 185 millimetre dressed price of [ $44.00 per lineal metre ex-GST](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) is higher than the listed Tasmanian Oak reference board, partly because the reference dimensions differ. Do not turn that into a universal species ranking. It is one current supplier list for particular sizes.

Blackbutt at [ $39.50 per lineal metre ex-GST for 19 × 185 millimetres](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) is a useful comparison for a warm Australian-hardwood brief. It can feel calmer than a heavily figured Spotted Gum in a broad panel, but the actual boards decide that. Both can be excellent on stairs and feature furniture where the visual variation is welcomed.

Tasmanian Oak is commonly selected for a lighter, more adaptable interior direction. The listed [ $30.25 per lineal metre ex-GST for 19 × 140 millimetres](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) makes it a useful reference price, but it is narrower than the listed Blackbutt and Spotted Gum boards. Victorian Ash is frequently discussed alongside it in interior joinery conversations; specify the exact product, supplier and grade to avoid a vague finish brief.

Jarrah and Blackwood offer deeper colour. They can be beautiful in a smaller furniture element, handrail or feature shelf, but can dominate a compact kitchen if used wall to wall. The right answer is often timber on the parts people touch, with a stable painted or veneer field around it.

## Imported and plantation options: American White Oak, European Oak, plantation pine

American White Oak is an imported material category, not an Australian native. Trend Timbers listed a 19 × 140 millimetre American Oak board at [ $30.55 per lineal metre ex-GST](https://trendtimbers.com.au/dressed-timber-price-list/) in August 2026. Its close price to the listed Tasmanian Oak reference board does not mean the finished joinery cost will be the same. Availability, grade, veneer selection, length and supplier all change the result.

European Oak is often sought for a particular pale, open-grain aesthetic, especially in veneer and furniture. Plantation pine can be appropriate in painted, secondary or budget-sensitive work where clear hardwood appearance is not the objective. The design mistake is using a species name as a status signal rather than asking whether it is suitable for the part being built.

A solid timber dining table has a different movement allowance from a timber veneer cabinet door. For [custom timber furniture](/custom-furniture), solid boards often make sense because the piece can be designed around them. For a long kitchen door, veneer over a stable substrate can be more controlled and still deliver genuine timber face and grain selection.

## Solid, veneer or engineered, and when each is correct

| Construction choice | When it is correct | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid timber | Treads, rails, furniture tops and tactile feature elements | Movement must be designed for and material use is higher |
| Timber veneer | Wide cabinet faces and consistent timber appearance | Edge treatment and veneer selection need care |
| Engineered timber-look finish | Budget-sensitive or hard-wearing broad cabinetry fields | Does not have the same repair or natural-grain character |
| Painted two-pack with timber accents | Kitchens needing stable colour plus a timber touchpoint | Requires deliberate material transitions |

Solid does not mean higher quality in every location. It means the material is solid through the part. Veneer does not mean fake timber; it is a real timber face over a stable substrate. That distinction is important. We cover finish-family trade-offs in the [kitchen finish comparison](/blog/2pac-laminate-timber-veneer-kitchen-finishes-nsw), so this guide stays focused on species and construction logic.

## Movement, humidity and coastal NSW

Timber moves with moisture. That is normal. Coastal Newcastle, the Central Coast, Coffs Harbour and Byron Bay add salt air and humidity to the maintenance conversation, while inland Orange and Bathurst can have different seasonal conditions. The design response is not to avoid timber. It is to allow movement in the joinery detail, use appropriate substrate and finish, and avoid trapping moisture against an unfinished edge.

A wide solid-timber panel fixed rigidly across its width can cup or split as conditions change. A well-designed piece allows it to expand and contract. In cabinetry, that may mean veneer on a stable panel for the broad door faces, with solid timber reserved for handles, shelves, end details or treads. The same thinking applies to [timber staircase joinery](/staircase-joinery).

## Finishing: oil, hardwax, two-pack over timber

Oil and hardwax products can retain a natural feel and are repairable in the right context, but they need a realistic maintenance conversation. A clear two-pack system can provide a more protective coating where the joinery is likely to receive water, grease and repeated cleaning. A two-pack colour finish over timber is a different visual choice: it can stabilise the look but covers the species that was selected.

Ask for a finish sample on the actual timber, not only a clear coat on a generic offcut. Oil can deepen colour. Matte coatings can read differently across boards. The kitchen lighting and benchtop undertone can turn a good workshop sample into a wrong room choice if nobody sees it in context.

## Sustainability, certification and what to ask for

Sustainability claims should be documented. Ask the supplier for species, country or region of origin, grade, and any available forest-certification or chain-of-custody information. A broad statement that timber is “sustainable” is not enough for a material decision. The relevant paperwork can vary by supplier and product, particularly between solid timber and veneer.

Also ask about yield. A joinery maker may need to order additional material to match colour and grain across a run or to avoid placing a poor board in the most visible door. That is not waste through carelessness; it can be part of achieving a coherent finished face. The quote should make the material specification clear.

## Costing a solid-timber kitchen against a two-pack one

Published market ranges put two-pack cabinetry at [about $1,800–$3,000 per linear metre installed](https://www.leadkit.com.au/blog/kitchen-renovation-cost-sydney-2026) and timber cabinetry at [about $2,200–$4,000 or more per linear metre installed](https://www.leadkit.com.au/blog/kitchen-renovation-cost-sydney-2026). For a six-linear-metre run, simple arithmetic makes that $10,800–$18,000 for the two-pack band and $13,200–$24,000 or more for the timber band. Those are cabinet bands, not full-kitchen quotes.

The economical option is often a hybrid: stable painted or laminate carcass and broad cabinet fields, then real timber where the hand and eye land. That might be a timber island end, shelves, a dining table or a stair rail rather than every door in the room.

## FAQ

### What is the best timber for kitchen cabinetry in Australia?

There is no single best timber. Spotted Gum and Blackbutt suit a durable Australian-hardwood look, Tasmanian Oak offers a lighter and often more even appearance, and American White Oak is selected for a recognisable imported-oak aesthetic. The right material depends on solid versus veneer construction, colour, movement, budget and the surrounding room.

### How much is Spotted Gum per metre?

At August 2026, Trend Timbers listed dressed NSW Spotted Gum at $44.00 per lineal metre for a 19 by 185 millimetre board, excluding GST. That is a timber input price for a specified board, not an installed joinery rate. Thickness, grade, waste, machining, finish and labour change the completed cost.

### Is Blackbutt harder than Tasmanian Oak?

Blackbutt is commonly selected where a harder Australian hardwood character is wanted, while Tasmanian Oak is often chosen for its lighter appearance and interior versatility. Do not select from a hardness claim alone. Grade, board width, moisture condition, finish and the intended use all affect how the joinery performs and looks.

### Does solid timber joinery move in coastal areas?

Yes. Solid timber responds to changes in moisture and humidity, including coastal NSW conditions. That movement is normal and has to be allowed for in board orientation, fixings, clearances and finish selection. Veneer over stable substrate can be the more controlled choice for wide door panels or large cabinet faces.

### Is American White Oak better than Tasmanian Oak?

Neither is universally better. American White Oak offers a familiar imported-oak grain and colour direction, while Tasmanian Oak is an Australian hardwood product with a different look and supply profile. Compare full samples in the room, then choose based on grain, colour, desired movement, budget and construction method.

### What certification should I ask for?

Ask where the timber comes from, what chain-of-custody or forest-certification information is available, and whether the species and grade on the quote match the sample. Certification availability varies by supplier and product. The important part is a documented supply path rather than an unsupported sustainability label.
