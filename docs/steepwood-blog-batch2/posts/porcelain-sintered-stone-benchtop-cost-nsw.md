## Why they are still legal in NSW

## FAQ

### How much is a porcelain benchtop in NSW?

Installed porcelain and sintered stone benchtops in NSW are commonly allowed at about $900 to $1,400 per linear metre, but the total depends on slab yield, cut-outs, edge detail and access. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.

### Is porcelain banned like engineered stone?

No. Porcelain without resin and sintered stone without resin are outside the engineered-stone definition used in the NSW prohibition; verify the selected product and fabrication method with the supplier. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.

### Is porcelain or sintered stone stronger?

Neither label alone settles the question. Compare the actual product’s thickness, edge treatment, substrate support, fabrication guidance and intended use rather than relying on a showroom description. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.

### Can porcelain chip on the edge?

Yes. Porcelain can chip, especially at exposed edges or where an unsupported impact occurs. Choose an edge detail suitable for the slab, and do not treat a thin premium surface like a solid timber workbench. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.

### What thickness should I choose?

Choose thickness with the edge profile, cabinetry support, appliance cut-outs and visual brief. A thinner slab can look sharp, while a thicker built-up edge can add a stronger visual line and more fabrication labour. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.

### Is porcelain cheaper than natural stone?

Porcelain may be cheaper or dearer than a natural stone selection depending on slab size, yield, edge work and fabrication. Compare installed totals and exclusions, not a material label or a square-metre showroom price. The useful next step is a measured layout and a written inclusion list, rather than selecting from a generic rate alone.
