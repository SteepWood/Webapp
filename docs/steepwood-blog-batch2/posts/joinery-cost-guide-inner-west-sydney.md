The right answer is a measured layout, a written access plan and a scope that separates joinery from the building work around it.

## The short answer

A terrace can take a sensible laminate kitchen, a compact laundry and wall-to-wall storage without becoming a full architectural rebuild. The expensive mistakes are predictable: ordering standard modules before the room is measured, moving services because an island looked good on a plan, or assuming a two-person installation has the same access as a suburban house.

## The terrace problem, stated plainly

Terraces concentrate several small constraints in one job. A masonry wall may be out of plumb. Floor levels may fall between the front and rear rooms. Existing service positions can be workable but not ideal. The kitchen may be one room in a chain rather than a square room with four clear walls.

I start by deciding what must be true on installation day: a clear carrying route, protected finished floors, a place to stage components, confirmed appliance specifications and a wall survey that allows for scribes rather than hopeful filler. That work is unglamorous. It is also the difference between a cabinet line that looks continuous and one that ends with a large, improvised panel.

A custom cabinet does not fix a structural issue. If a new opening, rear extension, floor levelling or plumbing relocation is required, it needs the appropriate designer and trades. Keeping the kitchen on its existing service wall is often the biggest cost control available.

## Measuring an out-of-square terrace, and why templating matters

A plan dimension is not a finished-wall dimension. We check several points across a wall, floor level, ceiling variation, door swings, window reveals and the point where an existing cornice or skirting must meet new work. Cabinet scribes and filler panels exist for a reason. They should be designed as part of the job, not discovered when the installer opens the boxes.

Benchtops need their own discipline. A long rear-wall run, an off-square corner and a visible return are precisely where a template after cabinets are installed protects the finish. For any porcelain, sintered or natural stone decision, confirm the current product and fabricator requirements before finalising the surface. Stone timing sits outside the cabinet measure.

## Galley kitchens, and what actually fits

A galley is successful when it moves people, hot cookware and appliance doors safely. A practical planning allowance is about 1,000 mm clear between opposing benches; [1,100–1,200 mm](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/policy-and-legislation/housing/designing-housing) is more forgiving in an active family kitchen. These are dimensional guides, not permission to ignore the actual room, particularly at fridge doors and dishwashers.

| Planning point | Practical guide | What goes wrong if ignored |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Clear galley passage | 1,000 mm minimum planning guide | Two people cannot pass safely |
| Comfortable shared passage | 1,100–1,200 mm planning guide | Appliance doors block circulation |
| Standard benchtop depth | 600 mm | A deeper run can consume the room |
| Tall-unit depth | 600 mm | Fridge and pantry doors need swing clearance |
| Corridor-laundry depth | about 650 mm including appliance allowance | Doors and hoses crowd the passage |

A 600 mm bench is a standard practice dimension. The rest of the layout is a trade-off. In a very narrow room, one full kitchen wall with a shallow storage or table wall opposite can work better than two full-depth banks. This is where custom earns its keep: not by adding features, but by choosing the right features to omit.

## Rear extensions and the joinery interface with new work

Rear additions can create the easiest kitchen room in a terrace and the most expensive one to correct later. The joinery needs a confirmed set-out for bulkheads, services, island clearances, windows, rangehood ducting and flooring thickness before manufacture. I prefer to see final appliance cut sheets and confirmed power positions before sign-off, not after cabinetry reaches the workshop.

The common failure is treating the kitchen as a late decorative layer. It is not. A 20 mm shift in a service, beam or window can change a tall-bank detail, an end panel or whether a pantry door clears an island. The answer is coordination, not a bigger contingency hidden in the quote.

## Laundries in corridors, under stairs and in cupboards

A good terrace laundry does not need a room of its own. It needs a defensible wet-area plan and cabinetry that makes the available width useful. The useful formats are a corridor run, a stacked appliance cupboard, an under-stair cabinet where head height permits, or a rear utility zone. The [Sydney laundry cabinetry page](/laundry-cabinets/sydney) is relevant if the scope is cabinetry; plumbing, electrical, ventilation, tiling and waterproofing remain separate trades unless they are specifically included.

For joinery, specify full-extension drawers where they solve a real storage problem, not by default. A [Blum LEGRABOX drawer system from $233 including GST](https://www.ekitchens.com.au/products/hardware-accessories/hinges) is a useful example of why a compact laundry can still have a material hardware allowance. One deep drawer may be more useful than two doors. Five premium drawers in a 1.8 m cupboard can simply consume budget and space.

## Storage in a house with no storage

Terraces reward boringly good storage: full-height entry cupboards, a shallow study wall, a robe that uses the ceiling height, drawers below a window seat where the structure permits, and pantry internals that let a shallow cabinet work. They do not reward every available wall becoming a cabinet wall. Too much joinery can make a narrow house feel narrower.

A robe should begin with the wall, the door opening and the user’s clothing. Then decide hinged versus sliding doors, hanging lengths, drawers and shelves. The [built-in wardrobes in Sydney](/built-in-wardrobes/sydney) service is the practical next step for a room-by-room storage scope. If a standard wardrobe system fits cleanly, it can be enough. If the ceiling falls, the wall is uneven or the room needs a desk and robe in the same line, custom provides a cleaner answer.

## Suburb by suburb

The detail that changes by suburb is usually access and stock, not the cabinetry brand. Newtown and Enmore often need a careful front-door route. Marrickville and Dulwich Hill can combine old cottages with later additions, so junctions deserve attention. Balmain, Birchgrove and Rozelle can bring narrow streets and stairs. Drummoyne may be a more open brick-house brief, yet parking and internal alterations still need checking.

No one should promise a crane, rear-lane delivery or flat-pack-in-place assembly without inspecting the job. These are tools, not default methods. Where a room cannot accept a large cabinet safely, units can sometimes be planned smaller and joined in place. That adds workshop and installation labour; it is not free, but it can protect walls, turns and finished floors.

## No side access: delivery, craning and flat-pack-in-place assembly

“No side access” means the installation plan starts at the kerb. We inspect parking rules, front steps, corridor turns, ceiling height, stair landings, internal doors and the route from vehicle to room. A standard-size tall pantry may fit the room but not the route. The correct response is to revise the module size or construction method before cutting board.

Craning can be appropriate for particular rear-access or upper-floor conditions, but it needs its own site, safety and permission assessment. It should never be sold as a casual line item. More commonly, well-planned cabinet sizes, a protected carrying route and staged delivery solve the access issue with less disruption.

## Conservation areas and development controls

The Inner West has [heritage conservation areas](https://www.innerwest.nsw.gov.au/heritage-and-conservation/heritage-conservation-areas), and Council publishes guidance for development on heritage-listed sites and within those areas. Whether internal joinery needs approval depends on the specific property and proposal, particularly if work affects significant fabric, services, openings or forms part of wider building work. Check the address early with [Inner West Council’s heritage and development guidance](https://www.innerwest.nsw.gov.au/heritage-and-conservation/heritage-and-development).

This is general information, not planning or legal advice. It is still worth doing before design sign-off because a period profile, reused original element or different service route can be a better outcome than forcing a generic kitchen into an old room.

### How do you fit a kitchen into a narrow terrace?

A narrow terrace kitchen works when the layout respects the clear walkway, appliance door swings and the existing service wall. A full site measure before manufacture is essential because old walls are rarely square.

### What is the minimum width for a galley kitchen?

A workable galley needs roughly 1,000 mm of clear passage between opposing benches, with 1,100 to 1,200 mm more comfortable where two people cook together. ### Can you deliver joinery with no side access?

### Where can a laundry go in a terrace?
