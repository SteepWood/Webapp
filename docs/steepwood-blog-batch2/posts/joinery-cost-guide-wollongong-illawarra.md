## Why there is no local cost information, and what that costs you

A price guide can do two things badly. It can publish a seductive low number without exclusions, or it can disappear into “contact us” language and leave every buyer to discover the variables alone. Neither helps when three quotes use different finishes, drawer systems and scope boundaries.

The better method is to establish a range, then identify the test. A low quote should survive questions about moisture-resistant board, number of drawers, appliance housings, access, pack-down, and any stone or trade allowances. A high quote should explain what it adds: 2-pac spray, timber veneer, integrated appliances, careful templating, internal fittings, awkward-site installation or design coordination.

[Cabinetry commonly represents 35–45% of a kitchen renovation total](https://www.leadkit.com.au/blog/kitchen-renovation-cost-sydney-2026). That is why the cabinetry line needs more detail than “kitchen supply”. It is also why retaining a workable layout can protect the total budget better than selecting a cheaper door.

## Escarpment sites, split levels and access

No standard percentage can accurately price an escarpment job. A ground-floor kitchen with kerbside parking may be simple. A room accessed via a stepped path, tight driveway or several internal levels asks for different handling, protection and labour. Those facts should be visible in the quote as an access allowance or a defined installation plan, not buried in a vague “site conditions” clause.

Measure the practical route: vehicle position, gates, stairs, ceiling height at turns, internal corners, room level, existing flooring, and a safe staging area. This is especially important for large tall units, stone coordination and pantry panels. A panel that fits the drawing but cannot turn through the hall creates a costly redesign.

This is where people waste money: locking in tall cabinetry before access has been checked. Breaking down or re-making an oversized component later is not an upgrade. It is a preventable variation.

## Salt exposure on the coastal strip

Salt air, wet gear and wind-driven rain do not change the basic truth that water ingress damages cabinetry. At Austinmer, Bulli, Kiama, Gerringong or Shellharbour, focus on moisture-resistant materials in vulnerable locations, appropriate hardware, carefully sealed edges and sensible distances from openings. Do not mistake a coastal palette for a coastal specification.

Timber veneer can be a fine feature, particularly away from splash zones. For hard-working kitchens and rental use, Polytec or Laminex can be the better finish: stable, cleanable and straightforward to replace. The choice is not budget versus quality. It is the correct material for the exposure and household.

### Heritage work and existing fabric

A character house is not a reason to freeze a kitchen in time, but it is a reason to understand the room before a modern design is imposed on it. [Wollongong Council advises that different rules may apply to heritage property work and offers planning and heritage guidance](https://wollongong.nsw.gov.au/plan-and-build/development-planning-rules/development-and-heritage-properties). Internal joinery may be straightforward; external changes, window alterations, an extension or significant original fabric can be a different conversation.

The practical joinery question is usually proportion. Retain a sensible relationship between ceiling height, door rail profile, existing skirtings and the new room rather than adding fake period detail everywhere. Take photographs of original architraves, vents and built-ins before demolition. If a heritage consultant or architect is involved, have the approved drawings and finishes settled before manufacture. That prevents an attractive but non-compliant detail being discovered when the kitchen is already on the bench.

## Apartments and strata approvals in Wollongong

An apartment kitchen is not just a smaller house job. [NSW Government guidance says kitchen or bathroom renovations need permission](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/strata/living/renovations); the actual by-laws and approval path belong to the owners corporation and the property. Check them before manufacture, not after a delivery slot is booked.

The joinery team needs the building rules in writing: lift size and booking, loading dock access, permitted work hours, floor protection, rubbish process, insurance documents, noise limits and a contact for the building manager. Most of this is administration, but it directly affects the installation plan. A service lift that only runs during a limited window may require a different delivery sequence from a freestanding house.

### A practical programme for an Illawarra installation

The most reliable Illawarra project starts by separating the room into decisions that must be made before manufacture and decisions that can wait. Before manufacture, lock the appliance models, sink, tap, benchtop thickness, finished floor level, power points, plumbing positions, rangehood path and any bulkhead dimensions. A tall fridge panel cut against an assumed appliance or a benchtop made before the cabinets are level is not a small administrative error. It is a remake risk.

For a split-level house, make a delivery map with the builder. Mark where vehicles can stand, where panels can be staged, which stairs are usable, who protects the floor and how old cabinetry is removed. For an apartment, attach the building manager’s instructions to the programme and reserve the lift before the installation date is promised. That sequence does not make the job bureaucratic. It allows the workshop to build component sizes that can actually enter the room and gives the owners corporation a clear plan.

The trade order matters. Demolition and any structural work come first. Then plumbing and electrical rough-in, wall repairs, flooring decisions and the final site measure where the project requires it. Cabinetry installs into a dry, stable, accessible room. Stone is templated only after base cabinets are installed and level. Appliances are fitted after the relevant service and panel details are confirmed. Painting and splashback work must be coordinated so neither trade damages a finished surface.

If a family needs to stay in the house, arrange a temporary food and washing-up plan before demolition, not during it. In an exposed coastal area, protect delivery days from wet weather where the access route or unfinished openings make it sensible. The goal is not a rigid calendar; it is a sequence that does not ask the installer to solve unfinished building work with cabinetry.

Before you approve the final order, nominate one person to answer site questions. On an Illawarra renovation that might be the homeowner, builder, architect or building manager, but it cannot be everyone by default. Confirm who approves a variation, who holds keys, where components can be left and when the electrical and plumbing trades will finish. That clarity is particularly valuable on a staged escarpment site, where a delayed handover can affect every following trade.
