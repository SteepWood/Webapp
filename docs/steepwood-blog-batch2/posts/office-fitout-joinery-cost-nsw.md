Office fitout cost per square metre is often quoted as [AU$3,011 per square metre](https://www.jll.com/en-au/insights/cost-fit-out-guide) in Australia, but that number does not tell a tenant representative what the joinery package costs. A tea point, reception, locker wall and meeting-room credenza can be a small portion of floor area yet a large portion of the coordination risk. This guide isolates the joinery line so an office manager, project manager or tenant representative can brief it properly.

The first rule is simple: do not budget joinery as a percentage until you know what joinery is in the brief. List every fixed element, locate its services and nominate its finish level. That changes a vague allowance into a procurement package.

## The joinery schedule a project team can tender

A tenderable schedule has one row per item and avoids adjectives in place of information. For each tea point, locker bank, credenza and reception, record the location, overall dimensions, substrate, finish, handles, hardware, internal fitout, benchtop, services, signage, delivery constraints and installation period. Attach the latest architectural plan and nominate which dimensions are site-verified. It is much easier to compare two proposals when both suppliers price the same doors, drawers, cut-outs and installation conditions.

Separate supplied joinery from adjacent trades. A tea point may need a plumber, electrician, tiler, stone fabricator, painter and base-building approval. A storage wall may need a fire-service review, data route or wall backing. A supplier can coordinate interfaces, but the project manager should name the party responsible for each approval. That prevents a completed cabinet being held up by an unapproved power point or an unavailable stone measure.

Include a variation protocol. Changes after shop-drawing approval can affect materials already ordered, nesting, paint batches, delivery modules and programme. A written variation that states the price, programme effect and drawing revision is not bureaucracy; it lets the tenant make an informed decision before work is changed.

## Budget controls that do not damage the workplace

The most reliable saving is simplification. Use a robust laminate for utility storage, reserve veneer or two-pack for a defined feature, standardise hardware where it does not affect function and avoid a different door or handle in every zone. Avoid false economy in the high-use locations: bins, tea point drawers, locker doors, reception worktops and AV access panels deserve durable hardware and serviceable construction.

Do not remove the service void, adjustable shelves or installation access to lower a quote by a small amount. Those details are cheap before manufacture and costly after handover. Likewise, avoid leaving material choices as “TBC” after tender. A provisional finish is an invitation for bids to diverge, and it gives the project team no dependable comparison.

Use the allowance structure to make trade-offs visible. If the reception front needs a premium veneer, nominate a laminate internal work zone. If the tea point needs a porcelain top, decide whether the adjacent storage needs the same finish. This is how a budget stays coherent: spend where performance and visitor experience warrant it, then simplify the rest.

## Handover, maintenance and end-of-lease planning

At practical completion, collect approved shop drawings, material and finish information, hardware adjustment instructions, key or lock schedule, warranty documents and a record of access panels. Facilities staff need to know how to remove a cable cover, reset a drawer front, clean a worktop and locate shut-offs or power routes. A small operations pack protects the asset more effectively than a generic email chain.

Before signing a lease, start an end-of-lease register for fixed joinery. Photograph the existing condition, record what was installed, preserve landlord approvals and keep the shop drawings. That record makes make-good conversations more factual. It also improves reuse opportunities: lockers and loose credenzas may be relocated, while a fixed tea point or reception needs a deliberate exit strategy.

A procurement workshop before tender is often worthwhile. Bring the tenant representative, designer, builder, services consultant and joinery supplier together with the plan on screen. Walk through the user journey: coffee, printing, meeting preparation, visitor arrival, secure storage, cleaning, IT support and after-hours shutdown. Each process exposes a small joinery requirement that can otherwise surface as a variation.

The resulting package should be realistic about its programme. Material samples, shop drawings and site measure are gates, not optional extras. When the team records them early, the joinery can be released with confidence and the landlord, contractor and tenant have the same expectations at handover.

The key is not to make every zone bespoke. Use custom work where the tenancy, workflow or access demands it, then use repeatable modules where they perform the same job. That approach protects both budget and maintenance without reducing the workplace to a catalogue layout.

## FAQ

### What does an office fitout cost per square metre in NSW?

The national office fitout benchmark is AU$3,011 per square metre, but it is not a Sydney or NSW quote. The final figure depends on building condition, scope, services, programme and market. Use the benchmark for first feasibility only, then separately price the joinery package and the base-building, design and trade scopes.

### How much of a fitout budget is joinery?

Joinery is not a fixed percentage of every fitout budget. It rises where the brief includes a reception, tea point, storage wall, lockers, meeting-room credenzas and bespoke acoustic or display elements. It falls in an open plan with loose furniture. Itemise the joinery package rather than applying a generic percentage to the whole tenancy.

### What is the difference between cat A and cat B fitout?

Cat A is generally the base landlord finish that makes an office tenancy services-ready, while cat B is the tenant-specific workplace fitout. Joinery usually sits in cat B because it responds to the occupier’s storage, hospitality, meeting and brand brief. The lease and building manual determine the exact division of responsibility.

### Who pays for make-good?

Make-good responsibility is set by the lease, negotiated agreement and any dilapidations schedule. It can require removal, reinstatement, retention or landlord approval of tenant joinery. Review the obligation before designing fixed elements, because a removable or reconfigurable solution may reduce end-of-lease cost.

### How long does a joinery package take to manufacture?

Joinery manufacture should be programmed after design sign-off, site measure, material approval and building access are confirmed. A standard reception product may show a 4–6 week lead, but a tenancy package includes more interfaces and cannot be assumed to follow that schedule. The supplier should provide a project-specific manufacturing and installation programme.

### Can joinery be installed after hours in a live building?

Yes, commercial joinery can be installed after hours if building management permits it and dock, lift, security, induction, noise and waste requirements are booked. The programme and cost must allow for those conditions. A tenant representative should obtain the building rules before the installation method and delivery modules are finalised.
