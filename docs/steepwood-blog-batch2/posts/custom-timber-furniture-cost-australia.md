That range is broad because furniture is not priced by a photograph. The maker is pricing timber selection, construction, finishing, handling and the way the finished piece gets into your home.

A retail table and a workshop-built table answer different questions. Retail spreads a fixed design across volume. A custom piece has to fit your room, chairs, family and material preference from the beginning. That does not make custom right for every purchase. It makes the details worth understanding before a quote arrives.

## Timber movement, breadboard ends and the details that stop a table cupping

Solid wood changes across the grain as humidity changes. That is normal material behaviour, not a defect to be defeated with screws. ## What a complete furniture quote should contain

A useful furniture quote names the finished item, not just the broad category. “Oak table” leaves open every cost driver: whether the top is solid or veneer, its finished length and width, the thickness, edge profile, base construction, colour, sheen, seats it is designed around and whether the base can be removed for delivery. The drawings do not need to be theatrical. A dimensioned plan, elevation and material schedule usually tell a buyer far more than a polished render.

The timber should be identified as a species and construction, with an agreed tolerance for natural variation. Solid boards can contain gum vein, colour change and figure; that is part of their character, but the client and maker should agree whether a very uniform look or a more expressive selection is wanted. Veneer is not a lesser material when it is correctly specified. It can deliver grain continuity and stability across a large sideboard or cabinet door, where wide solid boards would need a different construction.

Delivery is part of the scope. Confirm the street access, parking, stairs, lift, corridor turns, doors and who will receive the item. A long table may need a removable base or an alternate route; a tall sideboard may need feet fitted after it enters the room. That consideration prevents a finished piece becoming a site problem. It is also why a made-to-measure table can be the more economical outcome over its life: it is designed for the actual room, rather than forced into it.

## Care is part of the construction

Timber furniture needs ordinary, not precious, care. Wipe spills promptly, use placemats under hot or wet items, and avoid leaving a wet cloth or planter in one position. Strong household cleaners and prolonged direct heat can affect a finish, whatever the timber species. The manufacturer’s care instructions should match the finish selected, particularly for oil and hardwax systems that can be maintained differently from a lacquered surface.

Do not judge a table straight after a long freight trip or a dramatic seasonal weather change. Give it time to settle in a stable indoor environment. If a concern appears, photograph it, keep the room conditions sensible and speak with the maker before applying a repair product. The wrong repair can turn a small mark into a large refinishing job. This is not an argument against solid timber; it is the normal responsibility that comes with a responsive natural material.

## Choosing between custom and retail

Retail furniture is often the sensible answer for a temporary room, a tight budget or a piece that does not need to fit a particular space. Custom earns its cost where dimensions are awkward, the piece needs to match existing joinery, the timber choice matters, or the furniture will be used heavily for many years. The buyer should not apologise for choosing either path.

If commissioning a piece, avoid changing its scale after the maker has prepared a drawing. A 100 mm increase in length can alter timber yield, handling, base geometry and freight class. It can be a worthwhile change, but it is not a cosmetic note. Compare quotations on finished dimensions, construction and delivery, then select the scope that genuinely suits the home rather than the lowest incomplete figure.

A final sample sign-off is worth the time. View the timber and finish beside the existing floor, paint and upholstery in normal light. It is easier to agree the warmth, grain and sheen before the finish is applied than to debate a completed table after delivery.

Dimensions should include the chairs, circulation and table extension, not just the empty tabletop. Measure the room after allowing the dining chairs to pull out.

That small check avoids a finished piece that is beautiful but awkward to live around every day.

Plan for chairs, rugs and door swings too.

## FAQ

### How much does a custom dining table cost in Australia?

A custom hardwood dining table in Australia commonly starts around $3,500 including GST and can pass $15,000 including GST as size, species, joinery, finish and delivery complexity increase. A made-to-measure price should name the timber, finished dimensions, edge detail, base, finish and freight basis. Without that scope, two table prices are not comparable.

### What timber is best for a dining table?

The best timber for a dining table is the species and construction suited to the room, use and desired colour. Tasmanian Oak gives a lighter, consistent look; Blackbutt and Spotted Gum bring more character and hardness; American White Oak is an imported option with a familiar pale tone. Correct drying, joinery and allowance for movement matter as much as species.

### How long does custom furniture take to make?

Custom furniture lead time depends on timber availability, design approval, workshop queue, finishing and freight scheduling. A maker should confirm a project-specific programme in writing after the design is approved rather than promise a generic date. Complex tables, matching benches and interstate delivery need more coordination than a single small console.

### Can you freight furniture interstate?

Yes, SteepWood can arrange furniture-freight delivery to Queensland, Victoria, Western Australia and South Australia. Freight must be assessed against the piece’s dimensions, weight, finish protection and delivery access. It is not a substitute for a local in-home installation service, so the quote should state the delivery method, protection and receiving arrangements.

### Why is bespoke furniture more expensive than retail?

Bespoke furniture costs more than retail because the maker purchases timber for one design, machines and fits the joints, sands and finishes the piece, and carries the design and handling time that mass production spreads across thousands of units. The useful comparison is not a retail sale price; it is the material, construction and service life delivered.

### Will a solid timber table crack?

Solid timber can move with humidity and may develop small changes over its life, but good design allows for that movement. Breadboard ends, suitable fixing methods, acclimatised material and a stable indoor environment reduce risk. A table should not be fastened across the grain in a way that prevents it expanding and contracting naturally.
