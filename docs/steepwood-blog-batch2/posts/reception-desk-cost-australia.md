An office manager or tenant representative should not use those figures as interchangeable benchmarks. One is a standard furniture product; the other includes design resolution, materials, services, site logistics and installation into a live building.

The reception desk is usually the first physical touchpoint in a tenancy. It must make visitors welcome, give staff a workable counter and leave the building manager with a safe, installable package. Start with the floor plan and service strategy, then decide how expressive the front needs to be.

## The four tiers, from stock to fully bespoke

A reception desk falls into four useful tiers. A stock straight counter is a furniture purchase; a configured commercial desk adapts standard components; a custom straight desk is designed around the tenancy; and a bespoke curved or branded installation is a small architectural joinery project. Confusing the tiers is why early budgets go wrong.

## Curves, and why a radius front doubles the labour

A radius front usually means more templates, more material handling, more machining and more finishing than a straight front. It can be the correct decision where it directs visitors, softens circulation or supports a brand language. It is poor value when it exists only to make an ordinary plan look expensive in a render.

## Materials: two-pack, veneer, solid timber, stone and solid surface

Two-pack provides a smooth painted face; timber veneer provides warmth with controlled stability; solid timber belongs where its character warrants care; laminate is a hard-wearing commercial choice; stone and porcelain can suit a working counter. ## How to brief the reception package before tender

A reception package should start with a single plan that identifies the visitor route, staff route, counter positions, security line, accessible interaction point, screens, intercom, cabling and adjacent wall finishes. The project manager should nominate who owns each interface. The joinery contractor can build a counter, but cannot quietly resolve a base-building fire service, an undocumented power outlet or a branding approval after the desk is fabricated.

Issue a finish schedule with physical samples where possible. A drawing that says “timber” is not a specification. It should say veneer or solid timber, species or approved substitute, grain direction, sheen, edge treatment and protective finish. A painted front should name the colour system, sheen and whether it is two-pack or another coating. A worktop should nominate material, thickness, upstands, cut-outs and whether a stone subcontractor is included. These are the decisions that let bidders price the same work.

Ask for a services diagram. Reception staff may need dual monitors, charging, EFTPOS, a phone, a visitor-management tablet, printer access, panic or security equipment and a low-voltage route. If those provisions are not located before the bench is made, the usual outcome is a late cut-out, visible cords or a cupboard that cannot be opened. A removable skirting or service hatch is modest cost during manufacture and high value during maintenance.

Programme should be tied to real approvals. Confirm final design sign-off, materials release, site measure, manufacture, building induction, delivery booking, installation window, defects review and handover. Standard desks may advertise [4–6 weeks](https://www.spokejoineryandcabinetry.com.au/straight-receptions), but that does not include a custom material sample round, site constraints or an out-of-hours installation. The honest lead time is the approved programme, not the earliest manufacturing number.

## Common budget mistakes

The first mistake is using a stock product price to set the budget for a custom counter. The second is allowing a single provisional sum for “reception” without drawings, services or material schedule. The third is forgetting the occupied-building costs: a loading dock booking, lift protection, security, noise controls, waste removal and the crew hours required to work inside the building’s permitted window. These items are not joinery decoration; they are what allow the physical desk to arrive safely.

Another expensive mistake is treating the counter front as the only visible surface. Visitors see the front, but staff live with the rear: knee zones, legroom, bags, drawers, lockable storage, printer access, cables and the monitor position. A front that curves beautifully can be a poor result if the internal worktop is too shallow or the chair cannot move. Make the back-of-house plan a formal part of the drawing review.

If the project requires a branded feature, freeze the logo and illuminated-element supplier before manufacture. Late artwork is not a minor change when it affects router files, inlay, wiring or the position of a removable access panel. Keep the reception counter architecture simple and put the branding where it can be maintained.

## Installation day, documented

Before delivery, circulate the approved shop drawings, installation method, delivery sequence and contact list. The building manager needs to know the arrival time, vehicle details, loading route and whether lift protection or after-hours security is required. The installation crew needs the clear dimensions of the lift, doors and corridor turns. A reception front sometimes needs to arrive as several modules; that split and the visible joint locations should be resolved in the workshop, not improvised in the lobby.

At handover, check every door, drawer, lock, cable hatch, light and worktop before protective film is removed. Photograph the finished item and record the cleaning products that suit the material. A small defects list immediately after installation is normal; it is far easier to close than a vague problem reported after tenants have moved equipment onto the desk. This is the practical discipline that keeps a high-visibility element from becoming a late programme risk.

For commercial planning, keep the reception desk separate from the general fitout rate. JLL’s [AU$3,011 per square metre benchmark](https://www.jll.com/en-au/insights/cost-fit-out-guide) is a national average, not a substitute for a joinery scope. The desk can be comparatively small in floor area but high in complexity and public scrutiny. It deserves its own drawing package and procurement decision.

The project team should also agree the cleaning, security and reset requirements before opening. A reception can be visually simple but operationally demanding: visitors need a clear path, staff need privacy for screens and documents, and facilities staff need access to power and removable panels. Building those requirements into the shop drawings makes the desk easier to run, not merely easier to photograph.

When the detail is right, the reception works quietly: visitors know where to go, staff have a durable work surface, and the tenancy can be maintained without dismantling the counter.

That is the outcome a properly scoped commercial joinery package should deliver from the first handover.

## FAQ

### How much does a custom reception desk cost in Australia?

A custom reception desk in Australia is generally an indicative $6,000 to $20,000-plus excluding GST, depending on size, shape, materials, services and installation constraints. A standard straight desk can cost less, while a curved, branded or stone-topped desk can exceed that range. Compare quotes on the counter layout, materials, power provisions, access and installation hours.

### How long does a reception desk take to build?

A reception desk should be programmed after drawings, finishes, building access and site measure are approved. Standard made-to-order straight reception desks are advertised with a 4–6 week manufacturing lead, but a custom desk has different detailing and coordination requirements. Confirm the actual manufacturing and installation programme in the written proposal.

### What height should an accessible reception counter be?

An accessible reception counter should be designed and verified against the applicable National Construction Code and AS 1428 requirements for the project. The correct configuration depends on the building, approval pathway and customer interaction. Have the building surveyor or access consultant confirm the dimensions and clearances before the joinery drawings are released.

### Are prices quoted with or without GST for commercial work?

Commercial joinery is commonly budgeted and quoted excluding GST, provided the basis is clear. The quote should state whether every line and total is ex-GST or including GST, then show the GST treatment consistently. Do not compare an ex-GST furniture figure with an inclusive custom-installation figure as though they were the same number.

### Can a reception desk be installed out of hours?

Yes, a reception desk can be installed out of hours when the building permits it and loading, lift, noise, security and waste rules have been booked. After-hours work needs a site-specific plan and can affect cost. Confirm access windows, induction, protective requirements and who will hand over the work area before manufacture is complete.

### What material is most durable for a reception front?

The most durable reception front depends on the traffic, cleaning regime and expected impact. Laminate is a practical high-use choice; two-pack gives a seamless painted result but needs edge protection; timber veneer needs appropriate finish care. Use stone or porcelain where a hard-wearing counter surface earns its cost, not merely as a decorative skin.
