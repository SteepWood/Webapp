## Pricing a joinery tender

Joinery pricing depends on board grade, finish, edge detail, hardware, complexity, access and program. Two tenders on the same drawings can differ because one joiner has priced risk the other has missed. Our post on [why joinery quotes differ](/blog/why-custom-joinery-quotes-differ-australia) explains this.

To get a sharper price:

1. Provide full joinery elevations, not just plans.
2. Include a finishes schedule with product codes.
3. Specify hardware brands and ranges.
4. Share the program and site access rules.
5. Note any after-hours work requirements.

## Working together on the program

The best results come when the joiner is involved early. A short design review at tender stage often finds savings in material choice, module sizes or detailing. During construction, a weekly update on shop drawings, manufacture and delivery dates keeps the builder in control. After install, a quick defects walk-through and fast close-out keep handover on track.

## Materials that hold up on commercial sites

Commercial joinery takes more punishment than residential work. Staff open the same drawers hundreds of times a week, cleaners use stronger chemicals and trolleys hit kickboards. We usually recommend high-pressure laminate on fronts and tops that see heavy use, compact laminate for lockers, wet areas and high-impact surfaces, and moisture-resistant board in every kitchenette and vanity. Edges are a common failure point, so we specify thicker edging on exposed edges and seal hidden edges near water. Hardware should be commercial grade, with full-extension runners rated for the load. Where an architect has specified a material we think will struggle, we raise it at shop drawing stage with an alternative, so the builder can decide before manufacture rather than after a defect.
