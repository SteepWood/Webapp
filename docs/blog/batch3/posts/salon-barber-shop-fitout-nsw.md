# Barber Shop Fitout NSW: Salon Costs, Stations and Joinery

Barber shops and salons are some of the most joinery-heavy small businesses on any high street. Every station is a workstation, a storage unit and a mirror wall, and every surface is hit with water, product and hair clippings all day. This guide covers barber shop fitout costs in NSW, station design, plumbing, retail walls, durable finishes and how to open on schedule.

SteepWood builds barber stations, reception counters and salon joinery in our Newcastle workshop and installs across Newcastle, Maitland, the Central Coast and Sydney.

## The barber station

| Element | What to plan |
| --- | --- |
| Width per station | Room for the chair to turn and the barber to move around it |
| Mirror | Size and height to suit seated clients; LED lighting around or above |
| Bench top | Durable, water- and product-resistant surface |
| Drawers | Clippers, combs, scissors and sterilisation items |
| Power | Outlets at bench height for clippers and dryers, plus a cord holster |
| Tool storage | Holsters or recessed holders to keep the bench clear |
| Footrest | Protect the joinery from boots with a metal kick plate |

Barbers spend all day at the station, so we design around how they work: dominant hand, tool layout and how they move behind the chair.

## Salon additions

Hair salons add more services:

- **Wash basins** with plumbing, hot water and backwash chairs.
- **Colour bar** with sink, storage for tubes and bowls, and good ventilation.
- **Dispensary** for colour mixing and stock.
- **Towel storage** and laundry area.
- **Retail wall** for products, often the highest-margin part of the business.

Plumbing positions must be fixed before joinery is drawn, because basins and colour bars are built around them.

## Materials that survive salon life

| Material | Where it works | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High-pressure laminate | Station fronts, tops, retail shelves | Durable and easy to clean |
| Compact laminate | Bench tops, colour bars, wet zones | Waterproof and very tough |
| Timber veneer or battens | Feature fronts and walls | Warm, premium look |
| Stainless kick plates | Base of stations | Resists boots and chair bases |
| Sealed edges | Everywhere near water | Stops swelling |

Compact laminate is a solid sheet with no substrate. It [resists moisture, stains and impact](https://www.laminex.com.au/brands/laminex/product-ranges/compact-laminate), and Laminex lists its XR grade as a good choice for hair salons.

## Reception and retail

The reception counter in a barber shop is small but busy. It needs space for the booking tablet or POS, card reader, product display and sometimes a small coffee or water station. Our [shop counter guide](/blog/shop-counter-display-joinery) covers counter design in detail.

A retail wall near the counter, with good lighting and adjustable shelves, helps sell styling products. Lockable glass doors suit higher-value items.

## Program and approvals

1. Lease signed and landlord fitout guide received.
2. Station count, layout and plumbing positions agreed.
3. Landlord or centre approval.
4. Shop drawings and finishes approval.
5. Joinery manufacture while plumbing, electrical and flooring are done.
6. Install, often over one or two days.
7. Mirrors, lighting and final clean.

Hair and beauty businesses may also need to meet local council health requirements for skin penetration or hygiene if they offer those services. Check with your council early, and confirm any [skin penetration notification requirements](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/environment/skinpenetration/Pages/default.aspx) with NSW Health guidance.

## Common mistakes

- Too many stations squeezed into the space.
- No power at bench height, leading to cords across the floor.
- Soft finishes that swell near water.
- Forgetting towel and stock storage.
- Retail wall placed where customers never walk.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a barber shop fitout cost in NSW?

Hair and beauty fitouts are commonly quoted around $800 to $1,500 per square metre, depending on the number of stations, plumbing for wash basins, lighting and finishes. These are indicative 2026 ranges, not a quote.

### What joinery does a barber shop need?

Most barber shops need barber stations with mirrors and storage, a reception counter, a retail product wall, a back-of-house area for towels and stock, and sometimes a waiting bench. Salons add wash stations and colour bars.

### What is the best material for barber stations?

High-pressure laminate and compact laminate are popular because they resist water, hair products and constant cleaning. Timber veneer or battens add warmth on fronts and feature walls.

### Do salons need plumbing to every station?

Barber stations usually do not need plumbing, but wash basins do. Hair salons need plumbing for basins and sometimes a colour bar sink. Plumbing positions should be fixed before joinery is drawn.

### How long does a barber shop fitout take?

A small barber shop with existing services can often be fitted out in a few weeks once design and approvals are complete. Joinery is made in the workshop while site works happen, which shortens the closure period.
