## Materials for commercial joinery

| Material | Where it works | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Melamine board (Laminex, Polytec) | Carcasses, general storage | Choose moisture-resistant board near water |
| High-pressure laminate | Reception fronts, benchtops, counters | Edge detail affects durability |
| Compact laminate | Lockers, wet areas, heavy-use tops | Needs specialist machining |
| Timber veneer | Reception, boardroom, feature walls | Needs good protection from knocks |
| 2-pac painted | Feature pieces, brand colours | Chips more easily than laminate |
| Stone, porcelain or solid surface | Tops in kitchenettes and counters | Separate fabricator lead time |

[Engineered stone has been banned in Australia since July 2024](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/silica/engineered-stone-ban), so tops are now usually natural stone, porcelain, sintered stone, solid surface or laminate.

## Common commercial joinery mistakes

Most problems on commercial joinery packages come from the same few gaps, and they are easy to avoid at tender stage.

- **No joinery schedule.** Without elevations and a schedule, every joiner prices a different scope and the quotes cannot be compared.
- **Finishes not locked in.** Changing a laminate colour after shop drawings are approved can push manufacture back by weeks.
- **Services not coordinated.** Power, data and plumbing positions need to be fixed before the carcasses are machined.
- **Stone tops left to the end.** Benchtops and counter tops are templated after install, so the fabricator must be booked early.
- **Access ignored.** Lift sizes, loading docks and stair access decide how big each joinery module can be.
- **Defects window too short.** Allow time after install for adjustments, handles and sign-off before the client moves in.

A short pre-start meeting between the builder, the joiner and the electrician solves most of these.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is commercial joinery?

Commercial joinery is the built-in cabinetry and timber work in non-residential spaces: reception desks, kitchenettes, vanities, counters, shelving, lockers, wall panelling and display units. It is usually built in a workshop to shop drawings and installed as part of a larger fitout or construction program.

### Do you work as a joinery subcontractor for builders?

Yes. SteepWood works directly for business owners and as a joinery subcontractor for builders and fitout companies. We price from tender drawings and schedules, prepare shop drawings for approval and install to the builder's program.

### What information do you need to quote commercial joinery?

Architectural or interior drawings, a joinery schedule or elevations, a finishes schedule, the site program and any hardware or appliance specifications. If details are missing, we list our assumptions on the quote so the price is clear.

### What materials are best for commercial joinery?

High-pressure laminate and quality melamine board are the most common because they are durable and easy to clean. Compact laminate suits wet or high-impact areas. Timber veneer and 2-pac are used for reception and feature pieces. The right choice depends on use, budget and cleaning needs.

### How long does commercial joinery take to manufacture?

Once shop drawings are approved and materials are confirmed, most commercial joinery packages are manufactured in a few weeks. Large packages are staged to suit the site program. Lead times for special laminates, stone and hardware should be checked at the start.
