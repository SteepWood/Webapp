# Kitchen Installation Newcastle: Choosing Local Kitchen Installers

A kitchen is only as good as its installation. The best doors in the world look wrong if the gaps are uneven, the benchtop rocks or the end panel does not meet the wall. This guide is about kitchen installation Newcastle homeowners can rely on: how local kitchen installers work, what Newcastle homes throw at them, what it costs and what to check before you book.

SteepWood designs, builds and installs custom kitchens from our workshop in Newcastle, so we see the whole process from measure to the last handle.

## Why local installation matters

A kitchen installer who works in the Hunter every week knows the houses. Older weatherboard cottages, 1960s brick homes, new estates in Maitland and coastal homes in Port Stephens all need different approaches.

| Home type | Common Newcastle suburbs | Installation challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timber cottage | Hamilton, Mayfield, Wickham, Islington | Bouncy or sloping floors, walls out of plumb |
| Post-war brick | Adamstown, Lambton, New Lambton | Old services, small rooms, bulkheads |
| Character renovation | Merewether, The Junction, Cooks Hill | Heritage features, high ceilings |
| Lakeside home | Warners Bay, Belmont, Toronto | Moisture and humidity |
| New estate | Thornton, Chisholm, Gillieston Heights | Straight walls, but tight builder programs |

Each of these changes the install time and the materials we recommend.

## What happens on installation day

A custom kitchen install usually follows the same sequence, though older homes take longer.

1. Remove the old kitchen and check the walls and floor.
2. Mark a level line and set adjustable legs or a plinth.
3. Fix base cabinets and join them into straight runs.
4. Hang overhead cabinets and install tall units.
5. Scribe panels, fillers and kickboards to walls and floors.
6. Adjust doors and drawers, fit handles.
7. Benchtop templating, then benchtop fitting later.
8. Plumbing and electrical fit-off after the benchtop.

## How to choose a kitchen installer

Ask these questions before you book.

- **Who installs the kitchen?** Our own team, or a subcontractor?
- **Is the installation included in the quote?** Ask for supply and install on separate lines.
- **Which trades are excluded?** Plumbing, electrical, tiling, painting and appliances.
- **What licence and insurance do they hold?** Residential work over $5,000 needs a licence in NSW, and you can [check a licence with Service NSW](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/check-a-builder-or-tradesperson-licence).
- **What warranty applies to the install?** Check the cabinets and the fitting.
- **How are defects handled?** Ask how quickly adjustments are made after handover.

## Benchtops and installation timing

Stone, porcelain and sintered tops are measured after the cabinets are fixed, so the install happens in two stages. In between, the kitchen is usually usable with a temporary top or careful planning. Since [engineered stone was banned in Australia](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/silica/engineered-stone-ban), most clients choose natural stone, porcelain, sintered stone or laminate. ## Living through a kitchen installation

- Set up a temporary kitchen with a microwave, kettle and fridge in another room.
- Clear the kitchen and nearby areas before the install day.
- Plan for a few days without a sink between demolition and plumbing fit-off.
- Keep pets and children away from the work area.
- Agree a daily start time with the installer.

## Questions to ask at the measure

The measure is the moment that decides how smoothly the install goes. Use it well.

- Where will the sink, cooktop and dishwasher sit, and do the plumbing and power need to move?
- Will the rangehood vent outside, and how?
- Is the floor level, and will new flooring go in before or after the cabinets?
- Are any walls being moved or patched, and who is doing that?
- What appliances are you buying, and do you have the exact model numbers?
- How will the old kitchen be removed and disposed of?
- How will materials get into the house, and is there parking for the van?

Getting these answers early means the drawings are right, the trades are booked in the right order and installation day runs to plan. It also means your quote reflects the real job, not a guess.

## Caring for your kitchen after installation

A well-installed kitchen should only need small adjustments over its life. In the first few weeks, doors and drawers can settle slightly as the house moves with the seasons, so we come back to fine-tune hinges and runners if needed. Wipe laminate and 2-pac with a soft damp cloth and mild detergent, and avoid harsh cleaners on edges. Keep the area under the sink dry and report any leaks straight away, because water is the main enemy of board. Run the rangehood when cooking to reduce steam on overhead cabinets. Small habits like these keep a kitchen looking new for many years.
