# Composite Decking Central Coast: Composite vs Timber in 2026

On the Central Coast, a deck lives a hard life. Salt air in Terrigal, humidity around Brisbane Water, hot afternoon sun and bushfire zones in the hinterland all push materials to their limits. This guide compares composite decking Central Coast homeowners are considering with traditional hardwood, covering cost, maintenance, bushfire ratings and heat.

## Maintenance over ten years

The real comparison is not day one, it is year ten.

| Factor | Hardwood timber | Capped composite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Wash and scrub | Wash |
| Oiling | Regular re-coats to keep colour | Not needed |
| Greying | Turns silver grey if left uncoated | Colour holds, some fading |
| Repairs | Sand and re-oil scratches | Replace damaged boards |
| Splinters | Possible as boards age | None |
| Mould near water | Can occur on shaded decks | Can occur, but easier to clean |

On a 30 square metre deck, each oiling at NSW rates adds up quickly over a decade. For busy households or holiday homes in Pearl Beach or Avoca, composite often wins on time.

## Bushfire ratings on the Central Coast

Much of the Coast is bushfire-prone land, and the [Central Coast Council bushfire-prone land map](https://www.centralcoast.nsw.gov.au/plan-and-build/other-planning-considerations/bushfire-prone-land) is the starting point for any deck near bushland.

- Hardwoods such as [Blackbutt, Merbau, Spotted Gum, Silvertop Ash and Turpentine are listed as bushfire-resisting timbers for use up to BAL-29](https://harpertimber.com.au/timber-in-bushfire-prone-areas/).
- Some composite products are tested and rated from about [BAL-12.5 up to BAL-40](https://www.bigrivergroup.com.au/products/category/decking/), but many are not rated at all.
- Exempt decks on bushfire-prone land within 5 metres of a house must use [non-combustible materials](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/development-assessment/planning-approval-pathways/development-types/balconies-decks-and-patios).

Always confirm the BAL rating for your property before choosing boards.

## Heat, glare and comfort

Dark boards can get hot in full sun. On north- and west-facing decks in Kincumber, Erina or Wyong, a light grey or light timber-tone composite, or a pale hardwood like Blackbutt, is more comfortable for bare feet. Shade sails, a pergola or a roof over part of the deck also help.

## Subframes: the hidden part of every deck

Whichever board you choose, the subframe decides how long the deck lasts. Treated pine or hardwood joists, or steel subframes, must be sized and spaced to the board maker's rules. Composite boards often need closer joist spacing than hardwood. Good ventilation underneath is essential, especially on low decks near the ground.

## Approvals for Central Coast decks

Many small backyard decks are exempt. Under NSW rules a deck can be exempt if it is [no more than 25 square metres, no more than 1 metre above ground and at least 900 mm from the boundary in most zones](https://planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/development-assessment/planning-approval-pathways/development-types/balconies-decks-and-patios). Larger, higher or waterfront decks usually need approval.

## Which one should you choose

There is no single right answer, but the pattern is clear after many Central Coast measures.

Choose hardwood timber if:

- You love natural grain and are happy to oil the deck every year or two.
- The deck is partly shaded or under a roof, so weathering is slower.
- Your BAL rating suits a listed bushfire-resisting species.
- You want the option to sand and refresh the surface later.

Choose composite if:

- The deck is a holiday home or rental and nobody wants to maintain it.
- The site is exposed to salt spray, sun and rain all year.
- You want a consistent colour that will not grey.
- Children or pets play on the deck and splinters are a worry.

Many clients choose a mix: hardwood stairs and fascia with composite boards, or composite on the open area with hardwood under the roof. We can price both options side by side so you can compare.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is composite decking more expensive than timber?

Usually yes upfront. NSW timber decks are commonly quoted around $250 to $500 per square metre built and composite decks around $350 to $700 per square metre. Composite avoids regular oiling, so the gap narrows over time. These are indicative 2026 ranges, not a quote.

### Is composite decking good near the beach?

Quality capped composite handles salt air and moisture well and does not need oiling. The subframe and fixings matter as much as the boards, so use stainless steel fixings and a well-ventilated frame near the coast.

### Can I use composite decking in a bushfire zone?

Only some composite products have bushfire test evidence, with ratings ranging from about BAL-12.5 to BAL-40 depending on the product. Your BAL rating must be confirmed first, then a product with matching evidence chosen. Several hardwoods such as Spotted Gum and Blackbutt are listed as bushfire-resisting up to BAL-29.

### Does composite decking get hot?

Darker composite boards can get noticeably hot in full summer sun. Choose a lighter colour for north- or west-facing decks, or add shade. Light-coloured hardwoods also stay cooler than dark boards.

### How long does composite decking last?

Quality capped composite boards often carry long manufacturer warranties, commonly 20 years or more for the boards. Check exactly what the warranty covers, such as fading and staining, and follow the installation rules for the warranty to apply.
