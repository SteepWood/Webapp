# Office Kitchenette Joinery: Breakout Areas That Work in 2026

The office kitchen has become one of the most important rooms in the building. It is where people make coffee, eat lunch, catch up and sometimes meet clients. Good office kitchenette joinery makes that space work hard without looking tired after a year. This guide covers layouts, sizes, materials, appliances and costs for offices across Newcastle, the Central Coast and Sydney.

SteepWood builds office kitchenettes and breakout joinery in our Newcastle workshop and installs them as part of fitouts or as stand-alone upgrades.

## Why the kitchenette matters

Businesses bringing staff back to the office are investing in shared spaces. A tidy, well-designed breakout area encourages people to spend time together and gives visitors a good impression. A cramped tea point with one fridge and broken drawers does the opposite.

## Kitchenette layouts

| Layout | Best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-wall tea point | Small teams up to about 10 | Compact; sink, fridge, dishwasher, boiling water |
| Galley | Narrow rooms | Two runs facing each other; good workflow |
| L-shape | Medium teams | Extra bench space and storage |
| Run plus island | Larger teams and breakout areas | Island for eating, meeting and catering |
| Kitchenette plus bench seating | Multi-use breakout | Combines eating, meeting and casual work |

The right layout depends on headcount, lunch habits and whether the space is also used for events or client meetings.

## Sizing guide

| Headcount | Suggested setup |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 10 | 2.4–3.6 m run, one fridge, one dishwasher, boiling water tap |
| 10–30 | Longer run or L-shape, larger fridge, microwave space, good storage |
| 30 plus | Run plus island, two fridges, two dishwashers, multiple microwaves |

These are practical starting points. Peak times matter more than headcount: if everyone eats at 12:30, plan for the queue.

## Materials that last

| Element | Recommended material | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carcasses | Moisture-resistant board | Resists leaks and steam |
| Doors | High-pressure laminate | Durable, wipeable, many colours |
| Benchtops | Laminate, compact laminate, porcelain or natural stone | Choose by use and budget |
| Splashback | Glass, tiles or laminate | Easy to clean |
| Kickboards | Laminate or aluminium | Resists mop damage |
| Handles | Commercial-grade metal or finger pulls | Heavy daily use |

Since [engineered stone was banned in Australia](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/silica/engineered-stone-ban), office tops are mostly laminate, compact laminate, porcelain or natural stone. For busy kitchens, compact laminate is tough and water-resistant because it is a [solid, non-porous panel with no board core](https://duracube.com.au/article/what-is-compact-laminate/). It [costs more upfront than standard laminate](https://www.architectureanddesign.com.au/editorial/product-news/laminate-sheets-for-benchtops-cabinets-and-interiors-types-applications-and-care), but it lasts longer around sinks and dishwashers.

## Appliance planning

Most office kitchenette problems come from appliances that do not fit, overheat or have nowhere to go.

- **Fridges:** allow ventilation gaps per the manufacturer.
- **Dishwashers:** commercial or residential, with a nearby sink and bin.
- **Boiling and chilled water taps:** under-sink units need space and power.
- **Microwaves:** a dedicated shelf or housing at a safe height.
- **Coffee machines:** bench space, power and sometimes plumbing.
- **Bins and recycling:** pull-out bins under the bench save floor space.

Lock in appliance models before joinery is drawn so cut-outs are exact.

## Accessibility

A kitchenette should be usable by everyone. Consider a lower bench section, lever taps, D-handles rather than knobs, and clear floor space for wheelchairs. Your designer or access consultant can confirm requirements for your building.

## Breakout benches and seating

A breakout area works best when the kitchen and seating are designed together. A long bench at standing height gives people somewhere to eat, chat or open a laptop. Banquette seating along a wall with tables in front fits more people in less space and can include storage under the seat. Power points at bench level let people work there too. In smaller offices, an island with stools at one end can replace a separate lunch table altogether.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does office kitchenette joinery cost?

Kitchenette joinery is usually priced per linear metre of cabinetry plus tops, sinks and appliance housings. Custom cabinetry in NSW is commonly quoted around $650 to $1,400 per linear metre in quality board. A small tea point costs far less than a full breakout kitchen with an island. These are indicative ranges, not a quote.

### What is the best material for an office kitchenette?

Moisture-resistant board carcasses with high-pressure laminate doors and tops are the most common because they are durable, easy to clean and affordable. Compact laminate, stone or porcelain tops are used for heavier use or a premium look.

### What appliances should an office kitchenette have?

Most offices need a fridge, dishwasher, boiling and chilled water tap, microwave and sometimes a coffee machine. Larger teams may need two fridges or two dishwashers. Every appliance needs its space, power and ventilation planned in the joinery.

### How big should an office kitchenette be?

It depends on headcount and how people use it. A small office may need only a 2.4 to 3.6 metre run, while a larger team benefits from a longer run plus an island or bench for eating and meeting.

### Can the kitchenette be built while we keep working?

Yes. Joinery is built in our workshop and installed in a short window, often after hours, so disruption is minimal.
