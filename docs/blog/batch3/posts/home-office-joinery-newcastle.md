## Why a built-in desk beats a freestanding one

A freestanding desk is quick to buy, but it rarely uses the space well. A built-in desk can run wall to wall, use the full height of the room and fit around windows, bulkheads and skirting.

| Feature | Freestanding desk | Built-in home office joinery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit to room | Standard sizes only | Made to the measured wall |
| Storage | Separate units | Drawers, overheads and tall cupboards in one design |
| Cable management | Usually none | Grommets, channels and hidden power |
| Look | Furniture in a room | Part of the house |
| Resale | Leaves with you | Adds a functional study to the home |

Homes in older Newcastle suburbs such as Hamilton, Mayfield and Adamstown often have uneven walls and odd alcoves. Built-in joinery is scribed to those walls, so it looks like it was always there.

## Getting the sizes right

A home office that is the wrong height causes sore shoulders within a week. Desky's calculator suggests a [desk height band of about 635 to 700 mm](https://desky.com.au/pages/desk-height-calculator) for most adults, and ErgoExperts recommends at least [650 mm clear width for your legs](https://ergoexperts.com.au/what-is-the-correct-desk-size/).

Our working checklist:

- Desk height set for the main user and their chair.
- Desk depth of around 600 mm for a single monitor, more for dual screens.
- Clear knee space with no drawer directly over the legs.
- Overhead shelves high enough to clear the monitor and your head when standing.
- Drawers sized for A4 files if you keep paper records.

## Mistakes to avoid

A few small decisions make the difference between a home office you love and one you work around.

- Do not put a drawer bank where your knees need to go.
- Do not place the desk so a window sits directly behind your screen.
- Plan power above the desk, not just at skirting level.
- Allow ventilation for printers, routers and computers in closed cupboards.
- Leave space for a proper chair to roll back.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does home office joinery cost in Newcastle?

Built-in home office joinery in NSW commonly ranges from about $4,000 for a compact study nook to $12,000 or more for a two-person workspace with full storage. A single-wall desk and shelving run often sits around $5,500 to $8,000. These are indicative 2026 ranges, not a quote.

### What height should a built-in desk be?

Most adults are comfortable with a desk height between about 635 and 700 mm, depending on their height and chair. A custom desk can be built to suit the main user, and the chair and monitor should be adjusted around it. Allow at least 650 mm of clear knee width.

### Can a spare bedroom be turned into a home office with joinery?

Yes. A spare room can take a full-wall desk with overheads, or a desk on one wall and a fold-down bed or guest wardrobe on another. Planning the power, data and lighting before the joinery is built makes the room work far better.

### How do you hide cables in a built-in desk?

We plan grommets or cable trays in the desktop, a vertical cable channel behind a panel and a ventilated cupboard for the router or printer. Power points are positioned above the desk or inside the cabinet so nothing trails across the floor.

### How long does home office joinery take?

After the measure and approved drawings, a home office is usually manufactured in the workshop over a few weeks and installed in one to two days. Timing depends on board and hardware supply and the current workshop programme.
