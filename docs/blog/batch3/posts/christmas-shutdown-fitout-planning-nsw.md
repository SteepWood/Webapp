# Christmas Shutdown Fitout NSW: Plan Your 2027 Refit Now

A Christmas shutdown fitout is one of the smartest ways to refresh an office or shop without losing trading days. While staff are on leave and customers are away, joinery can be installed, walls painted and floors replaced, so the business reopens in January looking new. The catch is timing: design, approvals and manufacture must be finished before suppliers and certifiers close.

SteepWood builds and installs commercial joinery from our Newcastle workshop across Newcastle, the Central Coast and Sydney. This guide covers the key 2026–27 dates, what can realistically be done, lead times and a planning checklist.

## Working back from the shutdown

Joinery has to be designed, approved and built before the break. Progressive Corporate puts design and approvals at [six to ten weeks for small offices](https://www.progressivecorporate.com.au/fitout-hub/office-fitouts/how-long-does-an-office-fitout-take/) before construction starts. For a joinery-only refresh, the sequence is shorter but still needs planning.

1. **Scope and measure:** Agree what will change and measure the site.
2. **Quote and approval:** Itemised quote signed off.
3. **Shop drawings:** Drawings approved by you and the building manager or centre.
4. **Materials ordered:** Board, hardware and benchtops ordered before suppliers close.
5. **Manufacture:** Built in the workshop.
6. **Install window booked:** Dates confirmed in writing.

If you are reading this in early December, a full fitout over this break may be too tight, but a smaller joinery install may still be possible. Talk to us now, or plan for the next quiet period. ## Penalty rates and costs

Work on public holidays usually costs more because of award penalty rates, and fewer trades are available. Most shutdown works are scheduled on the normal weekdays between public holidays, such as 29 to 31 December, or in the first weeks of January. Building managers may also restrict works on public holidays.

## Building and centre rules over the holidays

- **Building managers:** Confirm access, security and lift bookings over the break.
- **Shopping centres:** Many centres restrict works during peak Christmas trade; ask for the centre's blackout dates.
- **Insurance:** Make sure certificates are current for the works period.
- **Security:** Plan who opens and locks up the site.

## Planning checklist

- Measure the space and photograph existing joinery.
- Agree a fixed scope and budget.
- Get itemised quotes for joinery and other trades.
- Sign off shop drawings.
- Request building manager or centre approval.
- Confirm install dates and access in writing.
- Arrange furniture moves and IT disconnection.
- Plan staff communication for the return date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a fitout be done over the Christmas shutdown?

Yes, smaller fitouts and joinery installs can be done while an office or shop is closed. The key is to finish design, approvals and manufacture beforehand, because suppliers, certifiers and many trades also close over the break.

### When is it too late to plan a Christmas shutdown fitout?

For the 2026–27 break, design and joinery orders generally need to be locked in during November or early December. If that window has passed, plan for the next quiet period, such as Easter or the June to July school holidays.

### What are the NSW public holidays over Christmas 2026?

In NSW, Christmas Day is Friday 25 December 2026, Boxing Day is Saturday 26 December with an additional public holiday on Monday 28 December, and New Year's Day is Friday 1 January 2027.

### Is work over the holidays more expensive?

Work on public holidays usually costs more because of penalty rates, and fewer trades are available. Scheduling work on normal weekdays between the public holidays, or in early January, often keeps costs reasonable.

### What should I do now to prepare for a January refit?

Measure the space, agree the scope, get itemised quotes, sign off shop drawings, request building or centre approvals and confirm delivery and install dates in writing before suppliers close.
