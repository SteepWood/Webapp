## Ergonomics

Comfort decides whether you actually use the office. Desky suggests a [desk height of about 635 to 700 mm](https://desky.com.au/pages/desk-height-calculator) for most adults, and ErgoExperts recommends at least [650 mm of clear knee width](https://ergoexperts.com.au/what-is-the-correct-desk-size/). Set the screen at eye level, allow room for the chair to roll back, and keep the keyboard close.

## Video-call ready

Hybrid workers spend hours on video. A few joinery choices help:

- **A tidy background.** Open shelves with a few books and objects, or timber panelling.
- **Closed storage** for files, printers and cables.
- **Good light.** Face the window, or add soft LED lighting under overheads.
- **Sound.** Soft furnishings and shelving full of books reduce echo.
- **Cable control.** Desk grommets and a vertical cable channel.

## Coastal finishes

Light timbers and soft colours suit Coast homes. Popular choices include:

- Light oak veneer or oak-look laminate.
- White, sand or soft green 2-pac.
- Textured laminates that hide fingerprints.
- Brushed or black handles with corrosion-resistant finishes.

In humid areas near the water, sealed edges and quality runners matter. The [home office joinery](/home-office-joinery) page shows the styles we build.

## Storage that keeps work separate

Working from home works best when work can be put away. Lockable drawers for client files, a closed cupboard for the printer and a shelf for daily items keep the room calm. If children share the room, give them their own drawers and shelves.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Placing the desk with a bright window behind the screen.
- No power at desk height.
- Fixed shelves that do not fit binders or books.
- Closed cupboards with no ventilation for equipment.
- Drawers where your knees should be.

## The commuter's home office checklist

If you split the week between a Sydney office and home, plan the room around the days you are there.

- A laptop dock and second screen so you can plug in and start quickly.
- A drop zone near the door or desk for bags, chargers and keys.
- Storage for the work items you carry back and forth.
- A good chair, because you will sit longer at home than you think.
- A door or sliding panel to close the office off at the end of the day.
- Blinds or sheer curtains to manage morning or afternoon glare.

Small details like these turn a spare room into a space you want to work in, and help keep work and home life separate on the days you do not travel.
