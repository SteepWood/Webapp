# Best Decking Timber NSW: Spotted Gum vs Merbau vs Blackbutt

Choosing the best decking timber NSW homes can use is a decision you live with for twenty years or more. The board you pick changes how the deck looks, how hot it gets, how often you oil it and whether it suits a bushfire zone. This guide compares the three most popular hardwoods, Spotted Gum, Merbau and Blackbutt, with treated pine as a budget reference.

SteepWood works with these timbers every week, in our Newcastle workshop for joinery and on site for decks and outdoor carpentry across Newcastle, the Central Coast and Sydney.

| Blackbutt | NSW and Queensland hardwood | Pale golden to light brown | [Bushfire-resisting to BAL-29](https://www.woodsolutions.com.au/publications/domestic-timber-deck-design) | Shows dirt and marks on pale boards |
| Merbau | Imported (South-East Asia and Pacific) | Rich red-brown, even | [Bushfire-resisting to BAL-29](https://www.woodsolutions.com.au/publications/domestic-timber-deck-design) | Tannin bleed, check certified sourcing |
| Treated pine | Plantation softwood | Pale, takes stain | Not a bushfire-resisting species | Softer, more movement |

WoodSolutions lists Blackbutt, Merbau and Spotted Gum among the species with an above-ground durability class 1 rating and a bushfire-resisting listing for decks up to BAL-29. Harper Timber's list also includes [Red Ironbark, River Red Gum, Silvertop Ash and Turpentine](https://harpertimber.com.au/timber-in-bushfire-prone-areas/).

## Spotted Gum

Spotted Gum is the workhorse of NSW decking. It is very hard, durable and handles exposed sites well. The grain has a gentle wave and the colour varies from pale grey-brown to a deep chocolate, which gives a natural, textured look. That variation is part of its character, but if you want a uniform deck, ask for a select grade.

Best for: exposed coastal and suburban decks, bushfire-prone sites up to BAL-29, homeowners who like natural variation.

## Blackbutt

Blackbutt is paler and more even than Spotted Gum. It suits modern coastal homes and lighter colour schemes, and it stays cooler underfoot in summer than dark boards. Pale timber shows dirt and dark marks more easily, so regular cleaning matters.

Best for: Central Coast and Northern Beaches coastal homes, contemporary designs, north- and west-facing decks.

## Merbau

Merbau has a rich red-brown colour and is stable, with less movement than some local hardwoods. It is imported, so check that the supplier can show certified, legal sourcing. Merbau bleeds tannin when it first gets wet, which can stain concrete, render and pavers below. Washing the boards before installation and planning for run-off helps.

Best for: homeowners who want a deep, even colour and accept early tannin management.

## Treated pine

Treated pine costs less and is easy to work, which makes it popular for budget decks and sheltered areas. It is softer, dents more easily and moves more with moisture. For high-traffic or exposed decks, hardwood or composite usually performs better.

## Board size, profile and fixing

- **Width:** 86 mm and 136 mm boards are common; wider boards move more.
- **Thickness:** 19 mm is common for residential decks on standard joist spacing.
- **Profile:** Smooth or reeded; smooth is easier to clean.
- **Gaps:** Allow for timber movement and ventilation.
- **Fixings:** Stainless steel near the coast; hidden fixing systems for a cleaner look.

## Finishing and care

Hardwood decks look best when oiled. A penetrating decking oil keeps the colour and slows weathering. In full sun, re-oil every year; in shade, every two years may be enough. Professional re-oiling in NSW commonly costs [$15 to $35 per square metre](https://www.thequoteyard.com.au/services/deck-staining-and-oiling-nsw). ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best timber for decking in NSW?

Spotted Gum, Blackbutt and Merbau are the most popular hardwood decking timbers in NSW. All three are durable above ground and are listed as bushfire-resisting timbers for use up to BAL-29. The best choice depends on colour preference, exposure, bushfire rating and budget.

### Is Spotted Gum better than Merbau?

Both are excellent. Spotted Gum is an Australian hardwood with a lively grain and varied colour, and is very hard. Merbau is an imported hardwood with a rich, even red-brown colour, but it bleeds tannin in its first months and should come from certified sources.

### Does Merbau stain concrete?

Yes. Merbau releases tannin when it first gets wet, which can stain concrete, pavers and render below the deck. Washing the boards before installation and managing run-off reduces staining.

### Is treated pine good enough for decking?

Treated pine is cheaper and suits sheltered decks, rentals or budget projects. It is softer, moves more and needs regular maintenance. For exposed coastal decks, hardwood or composite usually lasts better.

### How often should a hardwood deck be oiled?

Most hardwood decks need re-oiling every one to two years to keep their colour, more often in full sun. Re-oiling in NSW commonly costs around $15 to $35 per square metre if done professionally.
