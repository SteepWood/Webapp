## 2. The constraint that shapes the plan

Google ranks local results mainly on relevance, distance and prominence ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). A service-area business has one profile and at most 20 service areas ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en)). So:

- **Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Maitland, Port Stephens and the Central Coast** can be won with the Google Business Profile plus these posts, because distance helps.
- **Sydney** must be won through organic relevance: location pages, location-specific posts, reviews, citations and links. Batch 3 Sydney posts therefore target long-tail, small-business and builder-subcontract searches rather than head terms.

Keep the 20 GBP service areas set in Batch 2. Add the services Decking (once the licence scope is confirmed), Kitchen installation, Shopfitting and Commercial joinery to the GBP service list in week 1.

## 5. Local signals used in the posts

- Suburb and LGA names that match the reader's search (for example Honeysuckle, Charlestown, Erina, Tuggerah, Parramatta, North Sydney).
- A local condition that competitors skip: mine subsidence and coastal exposure in the Hunter, BAL ratings and salt air on the Central Coast, strata and after-hours access in Sydney.
- Links to the matching `/locations/...` page and, where it exists, the service-location page.
- `spatialCoverage` / `areaServed` in schema from `locationFocus`.
