## 1. Summary

1. **Batch 3 covered the three core regions at head-term level.** Newcastle, Central Coast and Sydney each now have office fitout, shop fitout, commercial joinery, kitchen installation, decking and home office posts. Batch 4 goes one level down: the largest sub-markets inside those regions that still have no SteepWood page ranking.
2. **Four new sub-markets were chosen from ABS data and competitor gaps:** Parramatta (commercial), Western Sydney industrial (Blacktown, Liverpool, Penrith), Lake Macquarie (kitchens and decks) and Maitland and the Hunter Valley (shops and kitchens). North Shore Sydney is added for premium home office joinery.
3. **Three new commercial verticals were chosen:** restaurants, pharmacies and childcare centres. Each has strong fitout spend, regulation that shapes the joinery, and almost no joinery-led content in search results. The ranking pages are fitout builders and cost estimators, not joiners.
4. **Search results for these sub-markets are led by directories and single-service local sites.** Localsearch, hipages and TradeRefer appear for Maitland and Lake Macquarie trade searches. A detailed local guide with cost tables, local rules and sources is rare.
5. **Every post supports an existing ranking page**, so the blog builds topical depth and internal links toward service × location pages instead of competing with them.

What the numbers say:

- **Western Sydney is the NSW commercial build engine.** Blacktown, Liverpool and Penrith together approved about $7.4 billion of non-residential building. Warehouses there all need offices and amenities.
- **Parramatta has a fitout opportunity created by vacancy.** The Property Council reports [Parramatta vacancy at 23.5 per cent, with B Grade at 39.9 per cent](https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/parramatta-vacancy-figures-highlight-need-for-cbd-flexibility). [Cushman and Wakefield](https://assets.cushmanwakefield.com/-/media/cw/marketbeat-pdfs/2026/q3/apac-and-gc/australia--sydney-metro-office-q3-2026.pdf?rev=3cdeb1eed5c542d686fd8ef522f8db48) puts Parramatta incentives around 34.4 per cent. Landlords are paying for fitouts to fill space. Colliers put vacancy at 22.1 per cent in H2 2025, JLL recorded positive absorption of about 5,300 m² in Q3, and [HTW](https://htw.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/HTW-Month-in-Review-July-2026-Commercial.pdf) notes almost no new Parramatta office supply in 2027.
- **Maitland and Cessnock build more new houses than Newcastle.** 704 and 585 against 257. [Maitland City Council](https://www.maitland.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-01/Annual%20Report%202024-25%20Final.pdf) reports more than 98,000 residents and 2.3 per cent annual growth. The [Newcastle Herald](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/9217589/chisholm-population-boom-drives-final-stage-of-waterford-estate) reports the Chisholm area has more than doubled in a decade. New estates mean kitchen upgrades and new local shops.
- **Lake Macquarie is the Hunter's largest residential market.** 798 new houses and $92.6m of alterations, with lakeside homes suited to decks and premium kitchens.
- **The North Shore spends on renovation, not new houses.** Willoughby, North Sydney and Ku-ring-gai together approved $345.4m in alterations. That suits premium home office joinery.

### 3.2 Competitor content patterns

- **Builders and fitout firms write cost pages; joiners do not.** In every new vertical, the ranking content comes from builders or estimators. A joiner with detailed item-by-item joinery content is unusual.
- **Directories win thin local queries.** For Maitland and Lake Macquarie trades, hipages, Localsearch and TradeRefer fill several page-one spots. Detailed local guides with tables and sources are the clearest way past them.
- **Few competitors cite regulation.** Posts that cite FSANZ, the National Regulations, NSW HPCA and council guides earn trust signals that thin service pages lack.
- **Old-brand advantage is local.** Osbornes (since 1977) and Hunter Kitchen King win on brand. SteepWood competes on workshop-built custom work, licence and warranty detail, and depth of content.

## 5. Keyword strategy

- **One primary keyword per post**, checked against all 66 live posts (see `KEYWORD-MAP.md`). No duplicates.
- **Pattern:** service + sub-market (Parramatta, Lake Macquarie, Maitland, Western Sydney, North Shore) or service + vertical (restaurant, pharmacy, childcare).
- **Placement:** title, H1, slug, meta description and the first 100 words, then naturally in two or more H2s and the FAQ.
- **Secondary keywords:** suburb variants (Charlestown, Warners Bay, Chisholm, Eastern Creek), phrasing variants (installers, installation, fitout, fitouts) and cost intent (cost, price, per m²).
- **Intent:** every post answers a commercial-investigation query (cost, process, rules, how to choose), then hands off to the service × location page and `/quote`.

## 6. Post structure (all 10)

1. H1 = title, with the primary keyword.
2. Opening paragraph answers the query directly in the first 100 words.
3. One short paragraph on SteepWood's workshop and service area.
4. Market or local context with sources.
5. Cost table with published ranges and links.
6. Process, rules or a decision table.
7. One `> **STEEPWOOD ...` CTA block linking to the matching service × location page and `/quote`.
8. Joinery list or local checklist.
9. "Working with SteepWood" with the trust line.
10. FAQ (5 questions, `[[FAQ]]` marker).
11. Closing H2 with "Get a free measure and quote" and the phone number.

Rules: 1,000–1,900 words, 6+ H2s, 2+ tables, 4+ inline sources, no "!", no emojis, no raw HTML, no `<digit`, Australian English, author Sukhveer Kaur.
