### The hidden services costs

- **Exhaust and ventilation:** commercial exhaust systems can cost [$15,000 to $45,000](https://petragroup.com.au/business-finance/restaurant-fit-out-cost-australia-2026/) depending on duct length.
- **Grease trap:** if there is none, allow [$10,000 to $25,000](https://petragroup.com.au/business-finance/restaurant-fit-out-cost-australia-2026/).
- **Council approvals:** DA and building approvals can cost [$5,000 to $15,000](https://petragroup.com.au/business-finance/restaurant-fit-out-cost-australia-2026/).

## Food premises rules in Newcastle

Every food business in Australia must comply with [Food Standard 3.2.3 Food Premises and Equipment](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2008B00577/latest). It requires premises, fixtures and fittings that can be easily cleaned, that keep out pests and that have proper water, waste, ventilation and lighting.

[City of Newcastle's design and construction guide for food premises](https://newcastle.nsw.gov.au/getmedia/229b1329-b7e5-495f-9c90-41f90f0cfa1e/Design-and-Construction-Guide-for-Designers-and-Operators-of-Food-Premises.pdf) sets out what council expects before you lodge a development application. It refers to AS 4674-2004 and the NSW Food Act 2003, and it makes points that directly affect joinery:

- Fixtures and fittings must be installed so they and the surrounding areas can be easily cleaned.
- Holes and inaccessible spaces must be sealed.
- No cavity construction is permitted in food areas.

In practice, that means joinery in kitchens and bar prep areas usually sits on legs or a sealed plinth, with impervious surfaces and sealed edges. It is cheaper to design this in than to rebuild after an inspection.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Signing a lease without checking exhaust and grease trap capacity.
- Designing joinery with sealed cavities in food areas.
- Ordering equipment after the joinery is drawn, so the sizes do not match.
- Using painted finishes on bar fronts and waiter stations.
- No contingency for unknowns in older Newcastle buildings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a restaurant fitout cost in Newcastle?

Published 2026 guides put a casual restaurant in a tenancy that already has kitchen services at about $1,800 to $2,600 per square metre, a mid-range conversion at $2,800 to $4,000 and premium dining at $4,500 to $7,000. These are planning ranges, not a quote.

### Is it cheaper to take over an existing restaurant?

Usually. One 2026 guide estimates that taking over an existing restaurant can bring a typical 220 square metre fitout down from $600,000 to $900,000 to about $300,000 to $450,000, because the exhaust, grease trap and services are already there.

### What food rules apply to a Newcastle restaurant fitout?

Food Standard 3.2.3, AS 4674-2004 and the NSW Food Act 2003. City of Newcastle publishes a design and construction guide for food premises that sets out its expectations before you lodge a development application.

### What joinery does a restaurant need?

Typically a bar or service counter, back bar, host stand, banquettes, waiter stations and storage. Front-of-house joinery is where most of the design budget shows.

Yes. We often supply and install the joinery package for a builder or designer, working from their drawings, and coordinate with the plumber, electrician and kitchen equipment supplier.
